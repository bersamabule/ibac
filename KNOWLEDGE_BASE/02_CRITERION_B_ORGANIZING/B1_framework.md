## What Criterion B Assesses

Criterion B evaluates a student's capacity for **organizational thinking**—the ability to structure ideas in ways that serve communication and argument. It measures the journey from accidental ordering to invisible, organic architecture.

---

## The Three Strands

### Strand (i): Organizational Structures
> "employ organizational structures that serve the context and intention"

**What this means:** Structure should be chosen strategically, not randomly or by default. The organization must serve the purpose of the writing.

### Strand (ii): Coherence and Logic
> "organize opinions and ideas in a coherent and logical manner"

**What this means:** Ideas must connect logically. The reader should be able to follow the progression of thought without confusion.

### Strand (iii): Referencing and Formatting
> "use referencing and formatting tools to create a presentation style suitable to the context and intention"

**What this means:** Formatting choices (headings, citations, layout) should support the communication purpose.

---

## The Structural Trajectory

Criterion B tracks the development of organizational logic:

| Level | Archetype | Type of Logic |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| 1-2 | The Collector | Associative logic (ideas by association) |
| 3-4 | The Constructor | Template logic (follows formulas) |
| 5-6 | The Architect | Rhetorical logic (structure serves purpose) |
| 7-8 | The Master Builder | Organic logic (form embodies meaning) |

---

## Level 1-2: The Collector

### Core Metaphor
A person with a basket, gathering interesting items and placing them inside with no organizing principle. The basket contains valuable things, but finding anything requires digging through everything.

### Type of Logic
**Associative logic.** Ideas connect by mental association—"this reminds me of that"—rather than by rhetorical purpose. The structure serves the writer's memory, not the reader's understanding.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- "Brain dump" organization—ideas in order of occurrence
- "And then... and then..." sequencing
- Paragraphs could be reordered without loss
- Missing or stated-fact thesis
- No transitions or only temporal connectors

### Diagnostic Markers
- **The Grocery List:** Points listed without hierarchy or connection
- **The Island Effect:** Paragraphs as isolated islands with no bridges
- **The Summary Trap:** Defaulting to chronological retelling
- **Arbitrary Sequencing:** No apparent reason for order

---

## Level 3-4: The Constructor

### Core Metaphor
An IKEA furniture assembler following instructions. All the pieces are there, assembled in the correct order, but there's no personal design—just faithful execution of a template.

### Type of Logic
**Template logic.** Structure follows a recognizable model (Intro-Body-Conclusion, Point-by-Point) but is applied mechanically without rhetorical awareness.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Clear introduction, body, conclusion structure
- Visible scaffolding ("First," "Second," "Third")
- Parallel but static sections
- Adequate but formulaic organization
- Transitions present but mechanical

### Diagnostic Markers
- **The Mechanical List:** Numbered points without interaction
- **Point-by-Point Template:** Structure determines content (not vice versa)
- **Additive Logic:** A + B + C rather than A → B → C
- **Formulaic Compliance:** All required containers present but not purposeful

---

## Level 5-6: The Architect

### Core Metaphor
An architect designing a custom building. The structure is purposeful—every room, every hallway serves the building's function. You can see the intelligence behind the design.

### Type of Logic
**Rhetorical logic.** Structure is designed to serve a specific argument. The order of ideas is itself part of the argument. Transitions are conceptual bridges, not just connective tissue.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Thesis-driven structure
- Order matters—paragraphs build on each other
- Strategic headers (analytical, not just topical)
- Integrated comparison (weaving, not alternating)
- Conceptual transitions linking ideas

### Diagnostic Markers
- **The Spectrum Argument:** Organization mirrors the analytical framework
- **Cumulative Logic:** Each section builds on the previous
- **The Reorder Test:** Paragraphs cannot be shuffled without breaking argument
- **Conceptual Bridges:** Transitions use ideas, not just conjunctions

---

## Level 7-8: The Master Builder / Rhetorician

### Core Metaphor
A master builder whose structures are so perfectly designed that the architecture becomes invisible. You don't notice the building; you just experience its effects. Form and content are inseparable.

### Type of Logic
**Organic logic.** Structure is not applied to content—structure IS content. The form embodies the meaning. Organization feels inevitable rather than chosen.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Structure invisible because it feels inevitable
- Form mirrors content (structure enacts meaning)
- Seamless flow—no visible joints
- Subverted expectations where appropriate
- Reader experiences meaning through structure

### Diagnostic Markers
- **Structural Mirroring:** Organization embodies the argument
- **Invisible Architecture:** Reader doesn't notice structure
- **The Inevitability Test:** Could this be organized any other way?
- **Meta-Structural Awareness:** Structure itself makes meaning

---

## The Compensation Rule: Structural Ceiling (B Limits A)

Chaotic organization caps how high analysis can be rated. If insights cannot be followed, they cannot be assessed as sustained or coherent.

| Criterion B Level | Maximum Criterion A Level |
|-------------------|---------------------------|
| Level 1-2 | Capped at Level 4 |
| Level 3-4 | Capped at Level 5-6 |
| Level 5-6 | No ceiling |
| Level 7-8 | No ceiling |

**Why this matters:** A student may have brilliant analytical insights, but if the disorganization prevents the reader from following those insights, they cannot be credited as "sustained" or "coherent" analysis.

---

## Diagnostic Questions for Criterion B

1. **The Reorder Test:** Could the paragraphs be shuffled without breaking the argument?
   - If YES → Level 1-2
   - If NO → Level 5-6+

2. **The Template Question:** Is the structure applied or designed?
   - Applied template → Level 3-4
   - Custom design → Level 5-6+

3. **The Transition Test:** What kind of logic connects sections?
   - Temporal ("Then...") → Level 1-2
   - Additive ("Also...") → Level 3-4
   - Conceptual ("This complexity extends to...") → Level 5-6
   - Invisible (seamless flow) → Level 7-8

4. **The Inevitability Question:** Could this be organized any other way?
   - Many alternatives seem equally valid → Level 3-4 or below
   - This structure feels necessary → Level 7-8

## Intervention Strategies

| Level | Strategy | Implementation |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| 1-2 → 3-4 | "The Skeleton" | Prohibit writing until graphic organizer is complete |
| 3-4 → 5-6 | "The Why Game" | Ask "Why this order?" for every section |
| 5-6 → 7-8 | "Form as Content" | Explore how structure can embody meaning |
