## Purpose

These diagnostic tools provide quick, reliable methods for calibrating assessment judgment. Use them to verify level assignments and identify performance patterns.

---

## Tool 1: The "So What?" Test

### Application
After identifying what a student has done in their analysis, ask: "So what? Why does this matter?"

### Level Indicators

| Level | Response Quality |
|-------|-----------------|
| **1-2** | Cannot answer. Student identifies features but cannot explain significance. |
| **3-4** | Answers with a basic, direct effect. "This creates suspense." "The reader feels sad." |
| **5-6** | Answers with a complex effect involving multiple elements. "This creates suspense by withholding information while the imagery suggests danger, putting the reader in the character's uncertain position." |
| **7-8** | Answers with a profound insight about human nature, knowledge, or meaning. "This reveals how narrative itself shapes our experience of time—we feel suspense because we are trapped in the character's present moment, unable to access the future we know exists." |

### Usage Example

**Student writes:** "The author uses a metaphor comparing life to a journey."

**Apply the test:** So what? Why does this metaphor matter?

- Level 1-2 response: [silence or repetition]
- Level 3-4 response: "It helps readers understand that life has different stages."
- Level 5-6 response: "It frames life as purposeful movement with a destination, which affects how readers interpret the character's struggles as progress rather than suffering."
- Level 7-8 response: "The journey metaphor imposes linearity on existence, revealing our cultural need to narrativize experience—the metaphor doesn't describe life so much as prescribe how we should understand it."

## Tool 2: The "Lens" Check

### Application
Identify what kind of "lens" the student is using to examine the text. The lens determines the depth and type of insight possible.

### Level Indicators

| Level | Lens Type | What It Sees |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| **1-2** | Magnifying Glass | Isolated bits—words, events, characters—examined individually without connection |
| **3-4** | Standard Glasses | The text clearly—features are identified and their basic functions understood |
| **5-6** | Microscope | Hidden structure and mechanics—how elements interact beneath the surface |
| **7-8** | X-Ray Machine | Through the structure to the philosophical skeleton—what the construction reveals about thought itself |

### Usage Example

**Text being analyzed:** Frost's "The Road Not Taken"

- **Magnifying Glass (1-2):** "There are two roads. One is grassy. The speaker chooses one."
- **Standard Glasses (3-4):** "The roads represent life choices. The grassy road represents the unconventional path. Frost is saying we should be individuals."
- **Microscope (5-6):** "The rhyme scheme creates formal order that contrasts with the thematic uncertainty. The speaker claims the choice 'made all the difference' but the poem's structure suggests this is retrospective meaning-making, not genuine distinction."
- **X-Ray Machine (7-8):** "The poem is less about choice than about the human compulsion to narrativize. The speaker cannot actually distinguish the roads, yet must construct a story of meaningful decision. The poem's cultural misreading as inspirational proves its point—we cannot resist imposing significance."

## Tool 3: The "Could Anyone Write This?" Test

### Application
Ask: Could any student following the assignment instructions produce this response? Or does it contain something distinctive?

### Level Indicators

| Level | Distinctiveness |
|-------|-----------------|
| **1-2** | Completely generic. Any student who read the text could produce this. |
| **3-4** | Follows expected patterns. A competent student following a template could produce this. |
| **5-6** | Shows individual thinking. This specific student's engagement with the text is visible. |
| **7-8** | Genuinely original. This insight could not have been predicted from the assignment. |

### The Checkbox Test
If the response reads like a checklist of expected elements, it's likely Level 3-4 at best. Higher levels require genuine engagement that goes beyond compliance.

## Tool 4: The "Strip the Style" Test

### Application
Mentally remove all stylistic flourish. What is the student actually arguing beneath the elegant language?

### Level Indicators

| What Remains After Stripping | Likely Level |
|------------------------------|--------------|
| Nothing substantive—just impressive-sounding assertion | Level 1-2 for analysis (regardless of language sophistication) |
| Basic, functional claims | Level 3-4 |
| Complex, multi-layered argument | Level 5-6 |
| Original insight with philosophical depth | Level 7-8 |

### The Polished Hollow Detector
If the work sounds impressive but you cannot paraphrase the actual argument in plain language, you may have a "Polished Hollow"—high style, low substance.

### Example

**Original:** "Shakespeare's intricate tapestry of linguistic virtuosity weaves a profound meditation on the ephemeral nature of human ambition."

**Stripped:** "Shakespeare writes about ambition."

The stripped version reveals that the original contains no actual analysis—just elaborate assertion. This is Level 1-2 thinking in Level 5-6 clothing.

## Tool 5: The "Evidence Ratio" Check

### Application
Count the ratio of claims to supporting evidence. Higher levels demonstrate claims with more precision and support.

### Level Indicators

| Level | Evidence Pattern |
|-------|------------------|
| **1-2** | Claims without evidence, or evidence without claims (quotes dropped in without explanation) |
| **3-4** | Basic evidence provided for main claims; some claims left unsupported |
| **5-6** | Consistent evidence for all claims; evidence is analyzed, not just cited |
| **7-8** | Evidence is precisely selected, thoroughly analyzed, and integrated into larger argument |

### The Dangling Claim Test
A "dangling claim" is an assertion without support. Level 5-6+ work has no dangling claims—every assertion is immediately grounded.

---

## Tool 6: The "Transition Test"

### Application
Examine the transitions between paragraphs and ideas. What kind of logic connects them?

### Level Indicators

| Level | Transition Quality |
|-------|-------------------|
| **1-2** | No transitions, or only temporal ("Then... And then...") |
| **3-4** | Basic transitions present ("However," "In addition," "Similarly") but mechanical |
| **5-6** | Logical transitions that build argument ("This complexity extends to..." "The same tension appears in...") |
| **7-8** | Seamless flow—transitions are invisible because the structure is organic |

### The Reorder Test
Could the paragraphs be reordered without loss of meaning? If yes, organization is Level 1-2. If reordering would break the argument, organization is Level 5-6+.
