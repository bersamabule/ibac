# Assessment Protocol: Two-Pass Grading Method

**Module:** `06_ASSESSMENT_PROTOCOLS/AP1_two_pass_grading.md`  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Last Updated:** 2025-12-05

---

## Purpose

The Two-Pass Grading Method prevents the Halo Effect by separating the assessment of "container" (organization, language) from "contents" (analysis, creativity). This ensures that good grammar doesn't hide bad thinking, and bad grammar doesn't obscure good thinking.

---

## The Core Problem

When reading student work in a single pass, assessors are vulnerable to:

**Positive Halo:**
- Beautiful sentences make weak analysis seem stronger
- Clear organization makes shallow thinking seem deeper
- Sophisticated vocabulary inflates perceived insight

**Negative Halo:**
- Grammatical errors make strong analysis seem weaker
- Unconventional structure makes creative choices seem confused
- Simple vocabulary obscures sophisticated thinking

The Two-Pass Method breaks this contamination by assessing container and contents separately.

---

## The Two Passes

### Pass 1: The Editor (Container)

**Focus:** Criterion B (Organizing) and Criterion D (Using Language)

**Questions to Ask:**
- Is it clear? Can I follow the argument?
- Is it structured? Does the organization serve the intention?
- Is the language appropriate? Varied? Accurate?
- Do mechanics support or hinder communication?

**Mindset:** You are an editor assessing the craft of communication, not the depth of thinking.

**What to Note:**
- Structural strengths and weaknesses
- Language patterns (vocabulary range, syntax variety, register)
- Mechanical accuracy
- Formatting and presentation

**What to Ignore (For Now):**
- Whether the ideas are good
- Whether the analysis is deep
- Whether the creativity is genuine
- Whether the interpretation is original

### Pass 2: The Critic (Contents)

**Focus:** Criterion A (Analysing) and Criterion C (Producing Text)

**Questions to Ask:**
- Is it smart? Is there genuine analytical insight?
- Is it creative? Is there authentic voice and risk-taking?
- Are claims justified with evidence?
- Does the thinking go beyond the obvious?

**Mindset:** You are a critic assessing the quality of thought and creativity, temporarily setting aside execution issues.

**What to Note:**
- Analytical depth and sophistication
- Evidence of synthesis and complexity
- Creative choices and their effects
- Original interpretation or insight

**What to Ignore (For Now):**
- Whether the grammar is perfect
- Whether the structure is conventional
- Whether the vocabulary is sophisticated
- Spelling and mechanical errors

---

## Implementation Workflow

### Step 1: First Reading (Pass 1 - Editor)
Read the entire piece once, focusing ONLY on B and D:
- Does the structure work?
- Is the language effective?
- Note observations for B and D assessment

### Step 2: Second Reading (Pass 2 - Critic)
Read the entire piece again, focusing ONLY on A and C:
- Is there genuine insight?
- Is there authentic creativity?
- Note observations for A and C assessment

### Step 3: Criterion Scoring
After both passes, assign levels:
- Score B and D based on Pass 1 observations
- Score A and C based on Pass 2 observations

### Step 4: Compensation Check
After initial scoring, check compensation rules:
- Does low B ceiling apply to A?
- Does low D obscure A?
- Is high D/C masking low A?

### Step 5: Final Adjustment
Adjust scores if compensation rules apply, and document the rationale.

---

## Why Two Passes Work

### Cognitive Load Management
Assessing all four criteria simultaneously overloads working memory, making contamination inevitable. Two passes distribute the cognitive load.

### Confirmation Bias Prevention
Once we form an impression, we seek confirming evidence. Separate passes prevent early impressions from contaminating later assessment.

### Fair Treatment of Diverse Learners
Students with language barriers (ELL, learning differences) deserve to have their thinking assessed fairly. Pass 2 ensures their analytical sophistication isn't penalized by Pass 1 weaknesses.

### Exposure of Hidden Patterns
Some students have high containers with low contents (The Polished Hollow). Others have high contents with low containers (The Thinker in Translation). Separate passes expose these patterns.

---

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Blending Passes
**Problem:** Allowing Pass 1 observations to contaminate Pass 2 assessment.
**Solution:** Physically separate the passes. Complete all Pass 1 notes before beginning Pass 2.

### Pitfall 2: Skipping Pass 1
**Problem:** Going straight to content assessment, unconsciously influenced by surface features.
**Solution:** Discipline. Always do Pass 1 first, even when you want to dive into ideas.

### Pitfall 3: Over-Adjusting
**Problem:** Applying compensation rules too aggressively, second-guessing valid assessments.
**Solution:** Trust the passes. Compensation rules are for clear cases, not marginal ones.
