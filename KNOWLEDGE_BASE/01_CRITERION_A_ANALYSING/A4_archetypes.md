## The Metaphor Sequence

The developmental trajectory for Criterion A is captured through four archetypes representing qualitatively different relationships to textual analysis:

---

## Level 1-2: The Summarizer / Tourist

### Core Metaphor
A tourist who visits a city, takes photos of landmarks, and returns home having "seen" everything but understood nothing about how the city actually works.

### Relationship to Text
**Describes content without analyzing craft.** The Summarizer can tell you what happens in a text but cannot explain how the text creates meaning. They are Thing-Finders—pointing at nouns (objects, people, events) rather than verbs (techniques, actions, effects).

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Retells plot or content
- Lists features without explaining function
- Offers personal reactions ("I liked it") as analysis
- Uses circular logic ("It's effective because it works")
- Missing analytical vocabulary

### The Broken Bridge
The Technique-Meaning Bridge is broken. The Summarizer sees that a metaphor exists but cannot traverse from device to meaning. They stand on one side, pointing at the other, but cannot make the crossing.

### Diagnostic Markers
- "The poem is about..."
- "The author uses [technique]" (with no explanation of effect)
- "This makes the reader feel..." (with no explanation of how)
- "I think this is relatable because..."

---

## Level 3-4: The Analytical Tourist / Tour Guide (Apprentice)

### Core Metaphor
A tour guide who has memorized the script—they can point out the landmarks and recite facts about them, but their understanding doesn't go beyond what's in the guidebook.

### Relationship to Text
**Points at techniques and explains their basic function.** The Tour Guide has learned that authors make choices for reasons. They can connect device to direct effect, but their explanations remain surface-level and formulaic.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Identifies techniques correctly
- Provides basic functional explanations
- Uses appropriate terminology (some of the time)
- Connects device to audience effect
- Inconsistent depth—strong moments mixed with thin analysis

### The Rickety Bridge
The Technique-Meaning Bridge is functional but shallow. The Tour Guide crosses but doesn't explore the territory on the other side. They state the effect without fully unpacking the mechanism.

### Diagnostic Markers
- "The author uses [technique] to create [effect]"
- "This shows that..."
- "The reader feels [emotion] because..."
- Correct but standard interpretations
- "Multiple purposes" mentioned but not deeply explored

### The Logic Gap
Level 3-4 students often exhibit the Logic Gap—stating cause and effect without explaining the mechanism. The connection is asserted rather than demonstrated.

---

## Level 5-6: The Analytical Tour Guide / Architect

### Core Metaphor
An architect who not only understands individual buildings but sees how they form a coherent cityscape—how the structures interact, how spaces flow into each other, how the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

### Relationship to Text
**Synthesizes multiple techniques into unified analysis.** The Architect understands that textual features don't operate in isolation. They analyze how elements interact, explore tension and paradox, and "peel back another layer" beyond the obvious function.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Weaves multiple techniques into single argument
- Explores tension and paradox
- Analyzes micro-level choices (word placement, syntax) for macro-level insight
- Consistent quality throughout—no valleys
- Every claim immediately supported
- Analyzes what is NOT there as strategic choice

### The Solid Bridge
The Technique-Meaning Bridge is sophisticated. The Architect not only crosses confidently but maps the territory beyond. They understand that multiple bridges connect technique to meaning, and they explore the network.

### Diagnostic Markers
- Synthesis language: "reinforces," "contrasts with," "creates tension"
- Analysis of absence: "The author's choice NOT to..."
- Micro-macro connection: small details supporting larger arguments
- Holding paradox: "Both X and Y are true simultaneously"
- No "dangling claims"—every assertion immediately grounded

---

## Level 7-8: The Perceptive Critic / Philosopher

### Core Metaphor
A philosopher who sees the city as a case study in human nature—who looks through the buildings to understand why humans build at all, what cities reveal about civilization, how architecture embodies worldview.

### Relationship to Text
**Analyzes texts as windows into worldview and epistemology.** The Philosopher treats the text as a case study in human thought, perception, or meaning-making. They analyze not just what the author does but what this reveals about how humans construct narrative, knowledge, or reality.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Meta-commentary: analyzes the text's awareness of its own construction
- Philosophical depth: connects choices to human nature, knowledge, meaning
- Original interpretation: offers insights beyond standard readings
- Granular micro-analysis fuels macro-level insight
- Analyzes reader complicity and interpretive responsibility
- Coins new terms or frameworks to capture observations

### The Transcended Bridge
The Technique-Meaning Bridge is transcended. The Philosopher sees the bridge itself as part of the meaning. They understand that the act of analysis is itself a meaning-making activity, and they can analyze that act.

### Diagnostic Markers
- Meta-language: "The text comments on its own construction"
- Philosophical vocabulary: "epistemological," "ontological," "recursive"
- Original conceptual frameworks: student coins terms to capture insights
- Reader implication: "The reader becomes complicit in..."
- Recursive irony: the text's meaning includes how it is (mis)read

---

## The Qualitative Shift: Level 6 to Level 7

The transition from Level 5-6 to Level 7-8 is not about doing the same things better—it's about asking different questions:

| Level 5-6 Questions | Level 7-8 Questions |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| How does this work? | What does this reveal about human nature? |
| What effect does this create? | Why do we respond to this effect? |
| How do these elements interact? | What does this interaction reveal about meaning-making? |
| What is the author doing? | What is the author assuming about knowledge and reality? |

---

## Progression Pathways

### From Summarizer to Tour Guide (1-2 → 3-4)
**Key development:** Learning that texts are constructed, not just experienced. The student must shift from "What happened?" to "Why did the author build it this way?"

**Intervention:** The Why Game—before writing, answer: (1) What technique? (2) What effect? (3) How does it create that effect?

### From Tour Guide to Architect (3-4 → 5-6)
**Key development:** Moving from isolated explanations to synthesized analysis. The student must learn to hold multiple techniques in mind simultaneously and analyze their interaction.

**Intervention:** The Web—draw connections between techniques, showing how they reinforce or create tension with each other.

### From Architect to Philosopher (5-6 → 7-8)
**Key development:** Shifting from analysis to insight—from "How does this work?" to "What does this reveal?" The student must develop meta-cognitive capacity.

**Intervention:** Permission to speculate—encourage interpretive risk-taking and original frameworks.
