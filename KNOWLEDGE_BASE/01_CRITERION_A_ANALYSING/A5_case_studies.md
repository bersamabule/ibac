## Module Purpose

This module contains 16 worked examples of student analytical writing, organized by achievement level (4 examples per level). Each case study includes:

- **Patient Profile**: Diagnostic label capturing the performance pattern
- **Student Work**: The actual student text (verbatim)
- **Diagnosis**: Achievement level assignment
- **Rationale**: Specific markers justifying the level, linked to diagnostic frameworks

Use these examples to:
1. Calibrate assessment judgment before evaluating real student work
2. Recognize performance patterns during assessment
3. Justify level assignments with reference to established precedent
4. Identify intervention priorities based on diagnostic markers

## Diagnostic Framework Reference

Before reviewing case studies, recall the cognitive trajectory for Criterion A:

| Level | Archetype | Core Characteristic |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| 1-2 | The Tourist | Identifies content but misses craft; broken Technique-Meaning Bridge |
| 3-4 | The Apprentice / Tour Guide | Functional explanation; connects device to effect but lacks depth |
| 5-6 | The Architect | Synthesis; explains how multiple features interact; holds tension |
| 7-8 | The Philosopher | Meta-cognition; analyzes worldview and epistemological implications |

## Part I: Achievement Level 1-2 (The Tourist)

### Diagnostic Definition

**The "Summary + Reaction" Trap.** The student distinguishes content (what happens) but misses craft (how it is built). They act as a "Thing-Finder," pointing to nouns (objects/people) rather than verbs (techniques/actions).

**Markers to Look For:**
- Retells plot or content without analyzing construction
- Identifies devices by name but cannot explain their function
- States personal reaction ("It made me feel sad") without textual justification
- Uses circular logic ("It's effective because it works")
- Missing analytical vocabulary (no terms for techniques)

---

### Case Study A1-01: Poetry Analysis (Frost)

**Patient Profile:** Minimal Identification

**Student Work:**

> "The Road Not Taken is about a person who has to choose between two roads... The road he picks is grassy and wanted wear. The poem makes you think about choices... The poet uses roads to mean choices... This poem is good because it teaches you to make your own choices."

**Diagnosis:** Level 1-2

**Rationale:**

- **Minimal Identification:** Identifies "roads" (noun) but misses the mechanism of the extended metaphor. Mentions "grassy and wanted wear" without explaining what this reveals about the speaker.

- **Logic Gap:** States the poem "makes you think about choices" (Effect) but fails to explain *how* Frost creates this effect via structure or language.

- **Unjustified Opinion:** Claims the poem is "good" and "relatable" based on universal truths rather than textual evidence.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Broken. Student sees the metaphor exists but cannot traverse from device to meaning.

---

### Case Study A1-02: Short Story (Jackson)

**Patient Profile:** Surface Description

**Student Work:**

> "The story takes place in a small town on a nice summer day. Jackson describes the flowers blooming... The story is surprising because usually lotteries are good but this one is bad... When you read it you don't expect what happens."

**Diagnosis:** Level 1-2

**Rationale:**

- **Failure of Intent:** Views the setting ("nice summer day") as factual reporting rather than a constructed contrast. Fails to explain *how* Jackson constructs the surprise.

- **Missing Vocabulary:** No use of terms like foreshadowing, irony, or juxtaposition.

- **Effect Without Mechanism:** Notes the surprise exists but cannot explain the technique that creates it.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Broken. Student experiences the effect but cannot reverse-engineer the craft.

---

### Case Study A1-03: Speech (Thunberg)

**Patient Profile:** Affective Response

**Student Work:**

> "She says 'How dare you' many times. This shows she is upset... The speech is for world leaders so she is trying to make them feel bad... You can tell she really cares... The speech is effective because climate change is important."

**Diagnosis:** Level 1-2

**Rationale:**

- **Symptom vs. Tool:** Identifies repetition ("How dare you") as a symptom of emotion ("she is upset") rather than a rhetorical tool (anaphora).

- **Circular Logic:** Claims effectiveness based on the importance of the topic, not the quality of the rhetoric.

- **Missing Rhetorical Vocabulary:** No mention of ethos, pathos, logos, anaphora, or other rhetorical terms.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Broken. Student conflates author's emotion with rhetorical strategy.

---

### Case Study A1-04: Comparative (Tan vs. Pew)

**Patient Profile:** Categorization

**Student Work:**

> "Amy Tan's story has characters and dialogue. The article has statistics and percentages. The story is more emotional... The article is more factual... The story is fiction and the article is non-fiction."

**Diagnosis:** Level 1-2

**Rationale:**

- **Grocery List Analysis:** Identifies features ("dialogue," "statistics") as items on a list rather than narrative tools.

- **Superficial Comparison:** Compares basic genre labels rather than how those genres create meaning.

- **Missing Synthesis:** No attempt to explain *why* the authors chose different approaches or what effects those choices create.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Broken. Student sorts features into categories without analyzing function.

---

### Clinical Note for Level 1-2

**Intervention Priority:** Epistemology & Inquiry

**Strategy:** Stop asking "What happened?" and ask "Why did the author build it this way?"

**The Why Game:** Before writing, students must answer:
1. What technique did the author use?
2. What effect does it create?
3. How does it create that effect? (the mechanism)

## Part II: Achievement Level 3-4 (The Tour Guide)

### Diagnostic Definition

**The "Adequate" Standard.** The student moves from identification to **functional explanation**. They act as an "Apprentice Mechanic," understanding that the author made choices for a reason and connecting the device to its direct effect.

**Markers to Look For:**
- Identifies techniques AND explains their basic function
- Uses appropriate terminology (some of the time)
- Connects device to audience effect
- Inconsistency: strong moments interspersed with under-supported claims
- Explanations are correct but not deep; misses nuance and complexity

---

### Case Study A2-01: Poetry Analysis (Frost)

**Patient Profile:** Functional Explanation

**Student Work:**

> "Frost uses the extended metaphor of two diverging roads to explore the theme of life choices... The poem's structure reinforces this idea... through its formal organization... The word 'perhaps' shows the speaker's indecision... This creates a somewhat ironic effect because the reader expects a clearer distinction."

**Diagnosis:** Level 3-4

**Rationale:**

- **Adequate Explanation:** Explains *how* the metaphor works (it explores choices) and *how* the rhyme scheme functions (creates order).

- **Audience Effect:** Identifies irony and explains why it affects the reader ("challenges common interpretation").

- **Limitation:** Explanations are functional but not substantial; misses the deeper contradiction between the stanzas.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Functional but shallow. Student crosses the bridge but doesn't explore the landscape on the other side.

---

### Case Study A2-02: Short Story (Jackson)

**Patient Profile:** Explaining the Obvious

**Student Work:**

> "Jackson establishes a false sense of security through her opening description... This pleasant imagery creates a pastoral mood that makes the violent ending more shocking... Jackson deliberately misleads the reader through this technique."

**Diagnosis:** Level 3-4

**Rationale:**

- **Intentionality:** Recognizes the author "deliberately misleads." Connects pastoral imagery to the shock effect.

- **Context:** Connects 1948 context to "conformity," moving beyond mere fact.

- **Limitation:** The analysis stays at the level of "obvious" effects; doesn't probe the deeper implications of reader complicity.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Functional. Student explains the what and why but not the deeper how.

---

### Case Study A2-03: Speech (Thunberg)

**Patient Profile:** Terminology & Function

**Student Work:**

> "Her most powerful rhetorical device is the repeated phrase 'How dare you,' which she uses as anaphora... This repetition serves multiple purposes. First, it creates an accusatory tone... Second, the phrase is emotionally charged... She balances this data with pathos."

**Diagnosis:** Level 3-4

**Rationale:**

- **Correct Terminology:** Uses ethos, pathos, logos, anaphora.

- **Multi-Functionality:** Explains that anaphora serves "multiple purposes" (tone, emotion, memorability).

- **Adequacy:** The analysis is correct but standard; it does not explore the specific nuance of the word "dare" or the power dynamics at play.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Functional. Student uses appropriate tools but applies them generically.

---

### Case Study A2-04: Comparative (Tan vs. Pew)

**Patient Profile:** Genre Awareness

**Student Work:**

> "Tan uses narrative and characterization to show the emotional complexity... while the Pew article uses data analysis... Tan's genre allows her to explore the psychological... statistics cannot capture."

**Diagnosis:** Level 3-4

**Rationale:**

- **Function over Form:** Explains *why* genre matters (narrative creates empathy; data creates scope).

- **Comparative Interpretation:** Notes similarities (both address formative experience) and differences in technique.

- **Limitation:** Comparison remains at genre level; doesn't analyze specific textual choices.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Functional. Student understands genre conventions but doesn't drill into specifics.

---

### Clinical Note for Level 3-4

**Intervention Priority:** Depth & Consistency

**Strategy:** Push beyond the first answer. When a student explains an effect, ask "And what else?" or "Go deeper."

**The Stamina Problem:** Level 3-4 students have the skill but lack the stamina for consistent rigor. Strong moments are interspersed with under-supported claims.

**Development Target:** From "What/How" to "Why does this matter?"

## Part III: Achievement Level 5-6 (The Architect)

### Diagnostic Definition

**Substantial Synthesis.** The student moves from "What/How" to **Internal Mechanics**. They analyze how multiple features interact to build complex meaning, often exploring tension and paradox. They "peel back another layer" beyond the obvious function.

**Markers to Look For:**
- Synthesizes multiple techniques into unified argument
- Explores tension, paradox, and complexity
- Analyzes micro-level choices (word placement, syntax) for macro-level insight
- Consistent quality throughout (no valleys)
- Every claim immediately supported; no "dangling claims"
- Analyzes what is NOT there as a strategic choice

---

### Case Study A3-01: Poetry Analysis (Frost)

**Patient Profile:** Synthesis & Tension

**Student Work:**

> "Frost's formal choices reinforce this theme of imposed narrative order. The consistent rhyme scheme... creates a sense of retrospective coherence that contrasts with the indecision... in the content... The 'undergrowth' carries connotations of obscurity... suggesting that future consequences remain necessarily hidden."

**Diagnosis:** Level 5-6

**Rationale:**

- **Synthesis:** Weaves metaphor, diction ("undergrowth"), and form (rhyme scheme) into a single argument about "retrospective coherence."

- **Micro-Analysis:** Analyzes enjambment physically ("forcing readers to 'bend' their reading").

- **Tension:** Holds the paradox between formal order and thematic indecision.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Sophisticated. Student not only crosses the bridge but maps the territory beyond.

---

### Case Study A3-02: Short Story (Jackson)

**Patient Profile:** Systemic Analysis

**Student Work:**

> "Jackson employs a strategic narrative technique of systematic misdirection... The euphemistic phrase 'civic activities'... demonstrates how bureaucratic language sanitizes violence... Jackson's use of free indirect discourse allows her to inhabit the community's perspective without endorsing it."

**Diagnosis:** Level 5-6

**Rationale:**

- **Complexity:** Analyzes "systematic misdirection" rather than just "surprise."

- **Technical Depth:** Explains *how* free indirect discourse creates "radical interpretive responsibility."

- **Language Analysis:** Connects bureaucratic language to thematic violence.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Sophisticated. Student analyzes the system, not just individual parts.

---

### Case Study A3-03: Speech (Thunberg)

**Patient Profile:** Rhetorical Architecture

**Student Work:**

> "Her rhetorical architecture deconstructs the performative activism... The speech's central rhetorical device... functions as both accusation and rhetorical structuring principle... The structural choice to withhold solutions is itself a rhetorical strategy... it positions world leaders as the ones with power."

**Diagnosis:** Level 5-6

**Rationale:**

- **Analyzing Absence:** Analyzes what is *not* there (solutions) as a strategic choice.

- **Audience Differentiation:** Explains how the speech affects skeptical vs. committed audiences differently.

- **Structural Analysis:** Sees the speech's organization as itself rhetorical.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Sophisticated. Student treats structure as content.

---

### Case Study A3-04: Comparative (Tan vs. Pew)

**Patient Profile:** Epistemological Comparison

**Student Work:**

> "Tan's fictional approach constructs meaning through specificity and interiority... The dual temporal perspective is genre-specific to fiction... Pew's genre-specific techniques include data visualization... creating a macro-level view unavailable to narrative fiction."

**Diagnosis:** Level 5-6

**Rationale:**

- **Genre Theory:** Analyzes "dual temporal perspective" as a specific affordance of fiction.

- **Consistency:** Every claim is immediately supported by analysis; no "dangling claims."

- **Epistemology:** Begins to explore how different genres create different kinds of knowledge.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Sophisticated. Student understands that genre shapes epistemology.

---

### Clinical Note for Level 5-6

**Intervention Priority:** Risk & Originality

**Strategy:** These students have moved from a sketch to a blueprint. They can hold two opposing ideas (tension) and analyze the friction between them.

**Development Target:** To reach Level 7-8, they need to move from analysis to insight—from "How does this work?" to "What does this reveal about human nature, knowledge, or meaning?"

**The Competence Plateau:** Level 5-6 students can execute everything correctly but safely. They need permission and encouragement to take interpretive risks.

## Part IV: Achievement Level 7-8 (The Philosopher)

### Diagnostic Definition

**Perceptive Insight / Meta-Cognition.** The student looks past the blueprint to the **worldview**. They treat the text as a case study in human thought, perception, or narrative. They analyze the philosophical/epistemological implications of the construction.

**Markers to Look For:**
- Meta-commentary: analyzes the text's own awareness of its construction
- Philosophical depth: connects textual choices to human nature, knowledge, meaning
- Original interpretation: offers insights not found in standard readings
- Granular micro-analysis fuels macro-level insight
- Analyzes reader complicity and interpretive responsibility
- Coins new terms or frameworks to capture unique observations

---

### Case Study A4-01: Poetry Analysis (Frost)

**Patient Profile:** Meta-Commentary

**Student Work:**

> "Functions as a meta-commentary on narrative construction itself... The verb 'sorry' introduces immediate interpretive complexity... its positioning before we know anything about the roads suggests the speaker's emotional response precedes his evaluation... This syntactic choice reveals... he laments limitation itself."

**Diagnosis:** Level 7-8

**Rationale:**

- **Perceptive Insight:** Analyzes the *placement* of "sorry" to reveal the speaker's pre-existing psychological state (granular micro-analysis fueling macro-insight).

- **Recursive Irony:** Recognizes that the "cultural misreading" of the poem is evidence of the poem's accuracy regarding human nature.

- **Meta-Textual Awareness:** Sees the poem as commenting on the nature of narrative itself.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Transcended. Student sees the bridge itself as part of the meaning.

---

### Case Study A4-02: Short Story (Jackson)

**Patient Profile:** Ethical/Meta-Textual Inquiry

**Student Work:**

> "Narrative strategy of complicit seduction... Jackson makes readers experience our own failure of moral imagination... The opening activates genre expectations... when the violent revelation arrives, it's not just shocking—it's genrically transgressive."

**Diagnosis:** Level 7-8

**Rationale:**

- **Meta-Textual Analysis:** Analyzes the reader's own complicity ("complicit seduction").

- **Theoretical Integration:** Seamlessly applies concepts like "reality effect" (Barthes) and "consonance" (Cohn) to explain *how* the manipulation works.

- **Ethical Dimension:** Moves from "what happens" to "what does this reveal about us as readers and humans?"

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Transcended. Student analyzes the reader as part of the text's meaning.

---

### Case Study A4-03: Speech (Thunberg)

**Patient Profile:** Moral Inversion & Prophetic Rhetoric

**Student Work:**

> "Strategic deployment of 'moral inversion rhetoric'... Thunberg inverts the age-based power hierarchy... The phrase 'How dare you'... is an archaic intensifier... positions climate inaction not as policy disagreement but as ethical violation... prophetic rather than deliberative rhetoric."

**Diagnosis:** Level 7-8

**Rationale:**

- **Conceptual Framework:** Coins "moral inversion rhetoric" to explain the power dynamics.

- **Register Analysis:** Analyzes the specific moral weight of "dare" and the shift from political to moral/prophetic registers.

- **Historical Context:** Places Thunberg within rhetorical tradition (prophetic vs. deliberative rhetoric).

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Transcended. Student creates new conceptual tools to capture the analysis.

---

### Case Study A4-04: Comparative (Tan vs. Pew)

**Patient Profile:** Epistemological Pluralism

**Student Work:**

> "Fundamentally incompatible epistemological projects... Tan employs 'presentational symbolism'... Pew uses passive construction... grammatically enacts its epistemological commitments... The comparison itself becomes an argument for epistemological pluralism."

**Diagnosis:** Level 7-8

**Rationale:**

- **Philosophy of Knowledge:** Analyzes texts as "ideographic" vs. "nomothetic" methods of knowing.

- **Grammar as Epistemology:** Analyzes passive voice not just as a style choice, but as an enactment of the research methodology.

- **Meta-Comparative:** The comparison becomes itself an argument about how we know things.

**Technique-Meaning Bridge Status:** Transcended. Student treats the comparison as a philosophical inquiry.

---

### Clinical Note for Level 7-8

**Recognition Note:** Level 7-8 is rare and exceptional. These students demonstrate:
- Original thinking that surprises even experienced teachers
- Ability to hold multiple interpretive frameworks simultaneously
- Willingness to take intellectual risks
- Capacity for meta-cognitive awareness

**Caution:** Do not expect Level 7-8 performance. Celebrate it when it appears, but recognize that strong Level 5-6 performance represents excellent achievement for Grade 8 students.

## Diagnostic Tools Summary

### The "So What?" Test

| Level | Response Quality |
|-------|-----------------|
| 1-2 | Cannot answer |
| 3-4 | Answers with a basic, direct effect |
| 5-6 | Answers with a complex effect involving multiple elements |
| 7-8 | Answers with a profound insight about human nature or knowledge |

### The "Lens" Check

| Level | Analytical Lens |
|-------|-----------------|
| 1-2 | Magnifying glass (looking at isolated bits) |
| 3-4 | Standard glasses (seeing the text clearly) |
| 5-6 | Microscope (seeing the hidden structure/mechanics) |
| 7-8 | X-Ray Machine (seeing through structure to philosophical skeleton) |
