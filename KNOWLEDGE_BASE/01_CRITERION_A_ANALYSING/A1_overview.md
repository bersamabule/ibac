## What Criterion A Assesses

Criterion A evaluates a student's capacity for **analytical thinking about texts**. It measures the cognitive journey from passive reading to active interrogation—from understanding *what* a text says to understanding *how* and *why* it creates meaning.

---

## The Four Strands

### Strand (i): Identification and Explanation
> "identify and explain the content, context, language, structure, technique and style of text(s) and the relationships among texts"

**What this means:** Students must recognize textual features (techniques, structures, stylistic choices) AND explain how they function. Identification alone is insufficient; explanation of mechanism is required.

### Strand (ii): Effects on Audience
> "identify and explain the effects of the creator's choices on an audience"

**What this means:** Students must bridge from technique to impact. The key question: "What does this choice DO to the reader/viewer/listener?" This is the Technique-Meaning Bridge.

### Strand (iii): Justification
> "justify opinions and ideas, using examples, explanations and terminology"

**What this means:** Claims must be supported with textual evidence and explained using appropriate analytical vocabulary. Assertions without evidence fail this strand.

### Strand (iv): Evaluation and Connection
> "evaluate similarities and differences by connecting features across and within genres and texts"

**What this means:** For comparative tasks, students must do more than list similarities and differences—they must evaluate the significance of those connections.

---

## The Cognitive Trajectory

Criterion A tracks the development of analytical thinking:

| Level | Archetype | Core Cognitive Mode |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| 1-2 | The Summarizer / Tourist | Identifies content; misses craft |
| 3-4 | The Analytical Tourist / Tour Guide | Functional explanation; connects device to effect |
| 5-6 | The Analytical Tour Guide / Architect | Synthesis; explains how multiple features interact |
| 7-8 | The Perceptive Critic / Philosopher | Meta-cognition; analyzes worldview and implications |

---

## Key Diagnostic Concept: The Technique-Meaning Bridge

The central diagnostic framework for Criterion A is the **Technique-Meaning Bridge**—the cognitive leap between identifying a technique and explaining its effect.

```
[TECHNIQUE] ----bridge---- [MEANING/EFFECT]
   "The author              "This creates a sense
    uses metaphor"           of isolation because..."
```

**Bridge Status by Level:**

| Level | Bridge Status |
|-------|---------------|
| 1-2 | **Broken** — Student sees technique exists but cannot cross to meaning |
| 3-4 | **Rickety** — Student crosses but explanation is shallow or formulaic |
| 5-6 | **Solid** — Student crosses confidently and explores the territory |
| 7-8 | **Transcended** — Student sees the bridge itself as part of the meaning |

---

## The "Summary + Reaction" Trap (Level 1-2)

The defining pattern of Level 1-2 analytical performance is the **Summary + Reaction Trap**:

1. Student summarizes what happens in the text (content)
2. Student offers personal reaction ("I liked it," "It was sad")
3. Student fails to analyze how the text creates meaning (craft)

**Example of the trap:**
> "The poem is about a man choosing between two roads. I think this is relatable because everyone has to make choices in life."

This response describes content and offers reaction but never analyzes Frost's craft.

---

## The Logic Gap (Level 3-4)

Level 3-4 students often exhibit the **Logic Gap**—they state cause and effect without explaining the mechanism:

**Gap present:**
> "The author uses dark imagery to create a sad mood."

**Gap closed:**
> "The author uses dark imagery—'shadows crept' and 'fading light'—which creates a sad mood because these images suggest things disappearing or being consumed, mirroring the character's sense of loss."

The difference: explaining *how* the technique creates the effect.
