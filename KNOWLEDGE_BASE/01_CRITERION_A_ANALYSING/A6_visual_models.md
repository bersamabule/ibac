## Purpose

These visual model descriptions serve as semantic anchors for the infographics designed to train pattern recognition in Criterion A assessment. They capture the structural and conceptual content of each visual, enabling the Expert System to reference visual frameworks even without direct image access.

---

## Infographic 1: Level 1-2 Pattern Recognition

**Title:** Pattern Recognition: Level 1-2 Analytical Performance

**Visual Structure:** Illustrates the "Pattern Gap" between Content and Craft. Shows a broken bridge with the student stuck on the Content side.

**Key Elements:**
- **The Broken Bridge:** Visual metaphor showing disconnection between Metaphor/Technique and Meaning/Effect
- **The Reporter:** Student figure pointing at "what happened" rather than "how it was written"
- **Surface Identification:** Shows student can identify elements but cannot explain function

**Diagnostic Patterns Illustrated:**
- Student engages in "Retell" rather than analysis
- Acts as "Thing-Finder"—focuses on nouns (objects, people) rather than verbs (techniques, effects)
- Has "Affective Response" but cannot differentiate intent
- Cannot integrate evidence beyond simple storytelling

**Use Case:** Quick calibration before assessing Level 1-2 work; visual reminder of what "broken bridge" looks like in practice.

## Infographic 2: Level 3-4 Pattern Recognition

**Title:** Pattern Recognition: Level 3-4 Analytical Performance

**Visual Structure:** Shows a student connecting a "Gear" (Feature) to a "Lightbulb" (Function) through adequate but shallow explanation.

**Key Elements:**
- **Gear-to-Lightbulb Connection:** Feature linked to function, but connection is simple
- **The Pattern Gap:** Space between "Adequate" and "Substantial" performance
- **Functional Explanation Zone:** Area where student operates

**Diagnostic Patterns Illustrated:**
- Links feature to function using analytical vocabulary
- Analysis is functional but lacks nuance and consistency
- Misses "Understanding Authorial Intent" regarding specific choices
- Analyzes features in isolation rather than synthesis
- Strong moments interspersed with under-supported claims

**Use Case:** Distinguishing Level 3-4 from Level 5-6; identifying the "adequacy threshold."

## Infographic 3: Level 1-2 to 3-4 Progression

**Title:** The Developmental Shift: From "WHAT" to "HOW"

**Visual Structure:** Side-by-side comparison showing transformation from Tourist to Tour Guide.

**Left Panel (Level 1-2 - The Tourist):**
- Focus: "Identification" (pointing at the thing)
- Question: "What is it?"
- Mode: "Thing-Finders"
- Lens: Content

**Right Panel (Level 3-4 - The Tour Guide):**
- Focus: "Functional Explanation" (explaining what it does)
- Question: "HOW does it work?"
- Mode: "Apprentice Mechanics"
- Lens: Craft

**Key Transition Marker:** Shift from describing content to explaining function.

**Use Case:** Understanding what development looks like; setting targets for Level 1-2 students.

---

## Infographic 4: Level 5-6 Pattern Recognition

**Title:** Pattern Recognition: Level 5-6 Analytical Performance

**Visual Structure:** Shows an "Architect" examining a blueprint that reveals internal mechanics of textual construction.

**Key Elements:**
- **The Blueprint:** Text visualized as architectural plan with visible systems
- **Internal Mechanics:** Connections between elements highlighted
- **Synthesis Zone:** Area where multiple techniques interact

**Diagnostic Patterns Illustrated:**
- Analyzes how multiple features interact to build complex meaning
- Explores tension and paradox within the text
- "Peels back another layer" beyond obvious function
- Consistent quality throughout—no valleys in analysis
- Every claim immediately supported; no dangling assertions

**Use Case:** Calibrating the "substantial" threshold; recognizing synthesis in action.

---

## Infographic 5: Level 3-4 to 5-6 Progression

**Title:** The Developmental Shift: From "HOW" to "WHY TOGETHER"

**Visual Structure:** Side-by-side comparison showing transformation from Tour Guide to Architect.

**Left Panel (Level 3-4 - The Tour Guide):**
- Focus: "Functional Explanation"
- Question: "How does this work?"
- Mode: Isolated analysis
- Lens: Individual techniques

**Right Panel (Level 5-6 - The Architect):**
- Focus: "Synthesis & Internal Mechanics"
- Question: "How do the parts work TOGETHER?"
- Mode: Integrated analysis
- Lens: System of techniques

**Key Transition Marker:** Shift from analyzing elements in isolation to analyzing their interaction.

**Use Case:** Understanding the synthesis threshold; coaching Level 3-4 students toward integration.

---

## Infographic 6: Level 7-8 Pattern Recognition

**Title:** Pattern Recognition: Level 7-8 Analytical Performance

**Visual Structure:** Shows a "Philosopher" looking through text to see human condition/worldview beneath.

**Key Elements:**
- **X-Ray Vision:** Student sees through text to philosophical skeleton
- **The Worldview Layer:** Deep structure beneath textual surface
- **Meta-Commentary Zone:** Area where text comments on its own construction

**Diagnostic Patterns Illustrated:**
- Analyzes *how* we think and construct meaning
- Engages in "Granular Analysis" (word-by-word precision fueling macro insight)
- Achieves "Epistemological Insight" (connects to human condition)
- Demonstrates "Theoretical Integration" (seamlessly applies frameworks)
- Shows "Relentless Rigor"—every micro-observation serves larger argument

**Use Case:** Recognizing exceptional performance; understanding what "perceptive" actually looks like.

---

## Infographic 7: Level 5-6 to 7-8 Progression

**Title:** The Developmental Shift: From "Construction" to "Philosophy"

**Visual Structure:** Side-by-side comparison showing transformation from Architect to Philosopher.

**Left Panel (Level 5-6 - The Architect):**
- Focus: "Internal Mechanics"
- Question: "How do the parts work together?"
- Lens: Construction (the text as system)
- Output: Analysis of textual mechanics

**Right Panel (Level 7-8 - The Philosopher):**
- Focus: "Worldview & Epistemology"
- Question: "What does this reveal about how we think?"
- Lens: Philosophy (the human condition)
- Output: Insight into meaning-making itself

**Key Transition Marker:** Shift from analyzing "Text" to analyzing "Human Condition"—from construction to philosophy.

**Use Case:** Understanding the qualitative leap to Level 7-8; recognizing meta-cognitive capacity.
