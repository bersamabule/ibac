---

## What Criterion C Assesses

Criterion C evaluates a student's capacity for **creative text production**—the ability to produce texts that demonstrate thought, imagination, and sensitivity. It measures the journey from completion-focused writing to genuine artistry with resonance beyond the immediate subject.

---

## The Three Strands

### Strand (i): Thought, Imagination, and Sensitivity
> "produce texts that demonstrate thought, imagination and sensitivity, while exploring and considering new perspectives and ideas arising from personal engagement with the creative process"

**What this means:** The writing should show genuine creative engagement, not mere compliance. Personal voice and authentic exploration matter.

### Strand (ii): Stylistic Choices
> "make stylistic choices in terms of linguistic, literary and visual devices, demonstrating awareness of impact on an audience"

**What this means:** Students should make intentional craft decisions and understand how those choices affect readers.

### Strand (iii): Relevant Details
> "select relevant details and examples to develop ideas"

**What this means:** Details should be chosen purposefully, not randomly. Specificity serves meaning.

---

## The Aesthetic Trajectory

Criterion C tracks the development of creative capacity:

| Level | Archetype | Relationship to Craft |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|
| 1-2 | The Checkbox | Completion without creation |
| 3-4 | The Paint-by-Numbers | Correct colors, visible lines |
| 5-6 | The Handcrafted Furniture | Skilled apprentice, solid work |
| 7-8 | The Artist / Alchemist | Takes risks, achieves resonance |

---

## Level 1-2: The Checkbox

### Core Metaphor
A student completing an assignment form—checking boxes, filling required fields, doing the minimum to satisfy requirements. No genuine creative engagement.

### Relationship to Craft
**Completion without creation.** The student produces text that meets technical requirements but shows no imagination, sensitivity, or personal voice.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Generic nouns ("the man," "a place")
- Tells instead of shows
- Formulaic language
- No sensory detail or specificity
- Writing sounds like anyone could have written it

### Diagnostic Markers
- **The Generic Test:** Are nouns specific or generic?
- **The Voice Test:** Could any student have written this?
- **The Investment Test:** Does the writer seem to care?
- **The Show/Tell Ratio:** How much telling vs. showing?

---

## Level 3-4: The Paint-by-Numbers

### Core Metaphor
A child with a paint-by-numbers kit. They're using real colors and the result looks somewhat like art, but the lines are visible, the technique is borrowed, and the personal touch is missing.

### Relationship to Craft
**Right colors, visible lines.** The student attempts literary devices and stylistic choices, but the seams are showing. Performance oscillates between creative "peaks" and functional "valleys."

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Attempts stylistic devices (similes, hooks)
- Inconsistent quality—peaks and valleys
- "Show, Then Tell" pattern
- Borrowed imagery (clichés, familiar phrases)
- Voice wavers between authentic and performative

### Key Diagnostic Framework: The Consistency Problem
Level 3-4 students produce **patchy work**—peaks of genuine craft surrounded by valleys of flat prose. Maintaining voice is cognitively exhausting, so they can sustain it only in bursts.

### The "Show, Then Tell" Pattern
Level 3-4 students often show something effectively, then immediately explain what they just showed because they don't trust the reader:

> "Her hands trembled as she reached for the letter. She was nervous." ← Undermines the showing

### Diagnostic Markers
- **The Consistency Check:** Is quality sustained or patchy?
- **The Trust Test:** Does the writer trust the reader?
- **The Cliché Count:** How many borrowed phrases?
- **The Seam Visibility:** Can you see where craft was attempted?

---

## Level 5-6: The Handcrafted Furniture

### Core Metaphor
A skilled apprentice crafting furniture. The work is solid, beautiful, and functional—clearly the product of learned skill. But it follows classic designs rather than innovating. You admire the craftsmanship without being moved by originality.

### Relationship to Craft
**Skilled apprentice work.** The text holds together on its own terms. Voice is sustained, details are organic, the reader trusts the writer. But there's a safety to it—competent execution without risk.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Integrity of voice throughout
- Specific, organic details (not inserted for effect)
- Writer trusts the reader
- Consistent tone and quality
- Solid craft, classic execution

### Diagnostic Markers
- **The Sustainability Test:** Is voice maintained throughout?
- **The Detail Quality:** Are details organic or inserted?
- **The Reader Trust:** Does the writer explain or let the reader infer?
- **The Risk Assessment:** Is the student playing it safe?

---

## Level 7-8: The Artist / Alchemist

### Core Metaphor
An artist who transforms materials into something transcendent. The craft is invisible because it serves vision. The work takes risks, achieves resonance beyond its immediate subject, and leaves the reader changed.

### Relationship to Craft
**Subversion and resonance.** The student uses text to probe the human condition. They take formal risks (second person, non-linear time, unconventional structure) and achieve philosophical depth. Form embodies meaning.

### Characteristic Behaviors
- Formal risk-taking that pays off
- Philosophical resonance beyond immediate subject
- Meta-cognitive control
- Craft serves vision (invisible technique)
- Reader experiences transformation

### The "Risk and Resonance" Gap
The key distinction between Level 5-6 and Level 7-8 is **Risk and Resonance**:
- Level 5-6 executes competently but safely
- Level 7-8 takes formal or thematic risks AND achieves depth beyond the immediate subject

### Diagnostic Markers
- **The Risk Inventory:** What conventions are subverted?
- **The Resonance Test:** Does meaning extend beyond the immediate?
- **The Transformation Test:** Is the reader changed?
- **The Invisibility Test:** Is craft invisible because it serves vision?

---

## Progression Pathways

### From Checkbox to Paint-by-Numbers (1-2 → 3-4)
**Key development:** Learning that writing involves craft choices, not just content delivery.
**Intervention:** "The Craft Hunt"—identify specific techniques in mentor texts before writing.

### From Paint-by-Numbers to Handcrafted (3-4 → 5-6)
**Key development:** Developing consistency and trust—sustaining voice and letting images speak.
**Intervention:** "The Explanation Ban"—forbid explaining what you've just shown.

### From Handcrafted to Artist (5-6 → 7-8)
**Key development:** Moving from competent execution to meaningful risk-taking.
**Intervention:** "The Risky Draft Policy"—cap safe work at Level 5; require risk for higher levels.

---

## The Risky Draft Policy

To incentivize intellectual and creative courage:

**Policy:** Cap "safe" work at Level 5. To achieve Level 7-8, students must attempt something difficult—even if they fail partially.

**Rationale:** This rewards courage over mere compliance. A flawed but ambitious piece demonstrates more growth potential than a flawless but safe one.

**Implementation:** Make this policy explicit. Students should know that perfect execution of conventional choices maxes out at Level 5-6.
