## Task Prompt

Analyze how F. Scott Fitzgerald presents the American Dream in The Great Gatsby. Examine the author's techniques and their effects on the reader. Support your analysis with specific evidence from the text.

---

## Student Response

In F. Scott Fitzgerald's seminal novel The Great Gatsby, the author presents a nuanced and multifaceted exploration of the American Dream and its profound implications for the characters who pursue it. Through sophisticated literary techniques, Fitzgerald crafts a devastating critique of 1920s society that continues to resonate with contemporary readers. This essay will examine three key aspects of Fitzgerald's presentation: the symbolic green light, the contrast between East and West Egg, and the characterization of Jay Gatsby himself.

The green light at the end of Daisy's dock serves as the novel's most prominent symbol of the American Dream. Fitzgerald positions this image at crucial moments throughout the narrative, establishing its significance from Nick's first observation of Gatsby "stretching out his arms toward the dark water." The green light represents Gatsby's hopes and dreams, connecting his personal desires to the broader American ethos of aspiration. By the novel's conclusion, the symbol has expanded to encompass all of humanity's striving for a better future.

Furthermore, Fitzgerald employs the geographical contrast between East Egg and West Egg to illustrate the social stratification of American society. The established wealth of East Egg, where the Buchanans reside, stands in stark opposition to the nouveau riche world of West Egg, where Gatsby has constructed his magnificent mansion. This spatial arrangement reflects the deeper divisions within the American class system, suggesting that the Dream's promise of social mobility may be more illusory than real. The author's careful attention to setting thus reinforces the thematic concerns of the novel.

The characterization of Gatsby himself provides the most compelling vehicle for Fitzgerald's exploration of the American Dream. Gatsby's transformation from James Gatz to Jay Gatsby embodies the self-invention that lies at the heart of the American mythos. His accumulation of wealth, his lavish parties, and his single-minded pursuit of Daisy all demonstrate the lengths to which individuals will go in pursuit of their dreams. Yet Fitzgerald also reveals the hollowness at the core of Gatsby's achievement, suggesting that material success cannot ultimately satisfy the deeper yearnings of the human heart.

In conclusion, The Great Gatsby remains a masterful examination of the American Dream and its discontents. Through his sophisticated use of symbolism, setting, and characterization, Fitzgerald creates a timeless critique of the pursuit of wealth and status. The tragic fate of Gatsby serves as a cautionary tale, reminding readers that the green light of aspiration, however beautiful, may lead only to disillusionment. Fitzgerald's novel thus stands as an enduring testament to the complexities of the American experience and the eternal human struggle between dreams and reality.

### Criterion A: Analysing — Level 2

**Rationale:**

Despite the sophisticated vocabulary and confident tone, this essay provides minimal genuine analysis. The student identifies techniques (symbolism, setting, characterization) but never explains HOW these techniques create meaning—only THAT they exist.

Consider the green light paragraph: "The green light represents Gatsby's hopes and dreams" is a statement of WHAT, not an analysis of HOW. The student doesn't examine Fitzgerald's specific language choices, the positioning of the symbol at chapter endings, the shift in what the light represents as Gatsby's relationship with Daisy changes, or the ironic tension between the light's beauty and its unattainability. They simply assert that the symbol is "significant" and "prominent."

The East/West Egg paragraph is even weaker: "This spatial arrangement reflects the deeper divisions within the American class system" is a claim without evidence or mechanism. HOW does geography reflect class? What specific descriptions create this effect? The student doesn't engage with Fitzgerald's actual prose.

The essay commits the classic Level 1-2 error of using analytical vocabulary ("nuanced," "multifaceted," "sophisticated") as a substitute for actual analysis. The phrase "Fitzgerald crafts a devastating critique" sounds analytical but explains nothing—what makes it devastating? How is it crafted?

Crucially, every observation in this essay could be found in a study guide. There is no original insight, no moment where the student notices something a casual reader might miss. The "So What?" test fails throughout.

**Strand Performance:**
- Strand i (identify/explain content, technique, style): Limited
- Strand ii (explain effects of creator's choices): Limited  
- Strand iii (justify opinions with examples): Limited

**Evidence:**
- "The green light represents Gatsby's hopes and dreams, connecting his personal desires to the broader American ethos of aspiration." → States meaning without explaining mechanism
- "Fitzgerald employs the geographical contrast between East Egg and West Egg to illustrate the social stratification" → Identifies technique but doesn't analyze HOW it works
